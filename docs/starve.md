# Starve

I was there when it happened, you see, the double suicide of the century. It was the dead of winter, but the two of them dressed for Fall. Here are the stills I took with my own eyes: her scarf purple and thin and clinging to her tall neck; his hat blue, both pairs of gloves red. For hours, or so I heard, they had stared at each other. I did not see the colours of their eyes, but the crowd around them must have. “Some kind of performance?” someone muttered to someone else. “No, this is not a show,” I said under my breath. Whispers, because the two of them were not saying anything, because it did not seem like they were even breathing. Finally, they moved. Up raised their gloved hands, reaching towards each other. He started crying. That was the first sound anyone heard from either of them—his sobs. She did not cry but her lips were trembling the same way her right hand was trembling over her left knuckles. One of her gloves dropped and the crowd reeled with it. Then another. Then both of his. I thought they looked like fallen maple leaves on the cobblestone. I still hear the screaming when their lips met and we all stirred at once. She kissed him. He kissed her back. We tried to force them apart, but they kissed and kissed and kissed until we put our arms around their throats and yanked, so hard that when they Erupted we went crashing to the ground. Just like that, they were gone. Each one of us were scrambling on our hands and knees to get away from the ashes, but I must have messed up, you see, I must have breathed it all in. It is everywhere now, the air I breathe is not like yours anymore. It is no longer light and fresh and bursting with different aromas. I cannot forget the smell of burnt souls. I must have messed up and tasted the ash, because after that day I could no longer get love out of my bloodstream. Not the kind of love that we know—the kind that we have only ever been warned about, the kind I witnessed that day. But, sweets, here is something I want you to know. I am tired of my gloves because I am obsessed with your skin. You know how it glows. You know how many times I have held your face between my gloved hands and touched your lips with my gloved fingers. You know how many breaths have dissipated into the space between our lips when we lie face to face. You know how many times you have leaned into the gap between us and death. You know how many times I have brushed your wild hair out of your eyes and tucked it behind your ears and wished I could feel the strands between my bare fingers. Your parents mutilated your skin. My parents painted mine with ink and needles. All of our parents did. They thought the jagged lines and notched edges would make us revolting, but they did not think it would make me want to feel the bumps of your scars all the more. There is something I want you to know. He had scars. She did, too. One time I told you it was selfish of me, of you, of both of us, to want. You told me it was selfish not to take a chance. As if the ashes knew selflessness. But, sweets, I am here now, and I am ready. We are lying face to face again, our lips only separated by a hair's breadth between us and death again, and I am brushing your wild hair out of your eyes and tucking it behind your ears again. The air smells like ashes, but the ashes no longer scare me because what I feel now, deep in my guts, is just like the paint and the scars on our bodies—unchangeable. I know nothing about selflessness, you see, but here is what I want you to know: I love you enough to touch. There are twisted lines and half-torn edges on the back of my hand but I stop caring about them when your hair slides between my bare fingers, and I forget they ever existed when you press your cheek to my palm. Somewhere below the last dark folds of my mind where you have yet to light up with the press of your sweet lips, I see the two of them—her scarf, his hat, their gloves, them—drift all the way up into the silver winter sky; but eventually, that still, too, disappears, burned through by colours and light. Just as they did. Just as we might.
