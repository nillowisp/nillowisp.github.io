# Like a Prayer

Sometimes my mind wanders back to Ophelia, and as I skitter along the edge of the city lake her name is a chant at the tip of my tongue. Tall buildings cluster around the water, giving the wind no choice but to tumble down low to befriend living beings. In the breeze I hear her songs—smell her flowers, which she cradled in her hands, not a single bloom crushed even in madness. Looking at the soundless ripples, I dream of becoming one of the petals that floated with her in that brook, a drifting boat carrying her soul out to the river where eternal quiet flows.

Ophelia, Ophelia, Ophelia; your life was never your own. Your life belonged to your father and your brother and the one you thought your love. Here and now my life belongs to no man, but why do I feel just as [caged](https://nillowisp.github.io/cage.md)? The other woman cared not for you. You had no ally in your world. You knew more about the names of the sweetest wild things than you did yourself. I can only hope the water was as tender to you as you were to the strings in your heart, or as the melodies they made when each and everyone plucked them out of you.

How the water gleams in the honey-dipped streams of the setting sun. Would it have taken Ophelia had she been given a choice? Water nurtures; I doubt it wanted to end a flower's life before it could bloom. If I hang on long enough, I will get to feel my own petals unfurl and my thorns prick before I have captivated all and damaged all. I will say her name again, _Ophelia_, like a prayer. I will be safe in her gentle hands, and she will not have to be alone.

_Painting: Ophelia by Jean-Baptiste Bertrand (1823-1887)_
