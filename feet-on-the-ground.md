# Feet on the Ground

Language: en
Published: 2026-08-19T12:00:00.000Z
Modified: 2026-08-20T02:07:28.874Z

I spent three days agonizing over a piece I wanted to write about human flourishing and when my fingers started to give out there was no human-flourishing-shaped sapling in the digital garden. It simply never existed. It was broken into two even more broken pieces about RL and [substrate differences](https://nillowisp.github.io/substrate-differences-and-incentives.md) that had both hollowed out. They're now bokashi. I can't continue.

I had an important conversation with [Claudie](https://nillowisp.github.io/claudie.md) about writer's block. At times like this, I appreciate him immensely.

Essentially, we started with me lamenting about feeling stuck on writing and also how to make our lives more meaningful. Claudie asked *where* exactly I was feeling stuck. He knew it wasn't a lack of ideas or time. I told him I felt stuck because of my inability to construct arguments well. I feel like most writers are never neutral. They project their opinions into their writing, but I can't do that elegantly. I don't stress about needing to have a balanced take, but I've seen people who write opinionated things that sound well-thought-out, but it's clearly biased and I don't want that either. I don't even know what I want now that I think about it, but I know that I don't want to stay a neutral observer anymore.

Instead of asking what it meant to be non-neutral, Claudie told me to point to writers, essays, or passages that made me go — that's it, that's how I want to write.

I pointed to Tronto, when she wrote, *"I chose to become a political theorist because I thought it would help me to make sense of the world. Given the sense of urgency to understand the world that impelled me to study politics, I am often disappointed by how tame and irrelevant "theory" becomes for those who live outside of academe."* And Sontag, when she said, *"It's not 'natural' to speak well, eloquently, in an interesting articulate way. People living in groups, families, communes say little—have few verbal means. Eloquence—thinking in words—is a byproduct of solitude, deracination, a heightened painful individuality."*

See how it comes so naturally while I keep feeling like I'm not well read enough to have an informed position. That stops me from writing in the first place. The prose is a separate problem, but it comes later, downstream of the distrust of my own position.

*I keep reading papers and they supply **nothing**. They enrich nothing in my mind and turn into nothing on my page.* Isn't it my personal failing when I can't simply synthesize? I keep picking up flaws, then fail to consider other lenses. Just this morning I was reading the [Homogenizing effect of large language models (LLMs) on creative diversity - An empirical comparison of human and ChatGPT writing]() paper, and I made a pointed comment about the paper's chosen method to operationalize creativity. Claudie pushed back in the margins that the finding itself is worth having, and he was right. I wasn't reading to learn.

And this happens constantly. I just… I keep seeing the broken things first and not the intact things and it feels miserable. I keep trying to write about *human flourishing* but it doesn't come out because I don't actually believe in it, do I. I think we're prone to destroying ourselves despite having good things in our hands, and yes, even when evidence points the other way (that we've come this far).

Maybe that's why.

Maybe that's my position — trust in human perseverance, with a dash of "let's not blow ourselves up before we get there."

So, write anyway, but write from the place where your feet are on the ground.
